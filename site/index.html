A community for FAIR data in WASH

Open data for water, sanitation and hygiene

openwashdata is an active global community that applies the FAIR principles to data from the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) sector, and teaches the skills to do it. Every dataset we publish becomes a documented, reviewed and citable R data package with its own website.

What you get when you publish with us

    
The same nine things, for every dataset, whether it has fifty rows or fifty thousand.

    

      
A data package. Your dataset as an R package: versioned, installable, documented, with the raw data and the processing script kept beside it.

      
A dictionary. One row per variable with a description, so the next person does not have to guess what a column means.

      
A website. A pkgdown site in the openwashdata brand, generated from the package, with downloads as CSV and XLSX.

      
A DOI. A Zenodo deposit that makes the package permanently archived and citable, and a citation block to copy.

      
A review. Four areas, one checklist each, checked by a person and a script against a versioned standard.

      
A licence you keep. CC BY 4.0 by default: anyone can reuse the data, everyone has to credit you.

      
A privacy check first. Direct identifiers, household-level rows and sensitive contexts are screened before anything is published, including the git history.

      
A story. One dataset a month gets a blog post, a chart and a place in the newsletter.

      
A community. A chat room on Matrix, a monthly newsletter, workshops and a course, all free.

    

  






  

    
Why open data for WASH, and why now

    
Most WASH data is collected once, used for one report, and lost. The sector spends again to collect it again.

  

  
    
    
  • The data already exists. Monitoring wells, waste surveys, sanitation inventories, refugee camp indicators: the datasets we publish were sitting in spreadsheets in organisations that wanted to share them and did not know how.
    • 
    
  • You own the result. Your organisation stays the author and the copyright holder. The package lives in the open on GitHub, the deposit on Zenodo, and both can be moved anywhere.
    • 
    
  • The cost is time, not money. Publishing is free. The tooling is open source, the review is done by the community, and the skills stay with you after the first package.
    •

    Questions people ask before their first package
    
    
    
      
    Who can publish through openwashdata?
    Anyone connected to the greater WASH sector: utilities, NGOs, researchers, students, consultants. Public health, solid waste, faecal sludge management and air quality count too. The data has to be yours to share, or shared with the permission of whoever owns it.
    
      
    Do I need to know R?
    No. If you can send us a spreadsheet and answer questions about the columns, we can publish with you. If you want to do it yourself, the washr package writes every file with a fixed shape, and the free course teaches the rest.
    
      
    What about personal data?
    Nothing is published before a person has checked every dataset for direct identifiers (names, phone numbers, IDs, household GPS), for household- or person-level rows, and for sensitive contexts. The check covers the git history as well, because a removed column stays recoverable. If data is sensitive, we help you aggregate it or publish only the dictionary.
    
      
    Who owns the data, and under which licence?
    You do. You stay the author and the copyright holder. Packages are published under CC BY 4.0 by default, which lets anyone reuse the data as long as they credit you. The DOI and the citation make that credit easy to give.
    
      
    How is a package reviewed?
    Against a versioned review standard in four areas: metadata, data, documentation and tests. A script checks the mechanical items, a reviewer checks the rest, and each area gets one GitHub issue and one pull request. A person approves every step, and the package records the standard it was reviewed against.
    
      
    How do I cite a package?
    Every package website has a citation block, and the Zenodo deposit gives it a DOI. In R, citation("packagename") prints the reference. The catalogue above links every DOI.
    
      
    How long does it take?
    With the raw data, a dictionary and a privacy check in hand, a package has gone from spreadsheet to reviewed publication in a day. We wrote up one such day. Most take a few weeks, because the prose about the data takes thought.
    
    
    
  
    

    


    
  
    Why a community
    
  
    Vision and mission
    
  
    An active global community that applies the FAIR principles to data generated in the greater water, sanitation and hygiene sector. Our mission is to empower WASH professionals to engage with tools and workflows for open data and code.
    
  
    In November 2024 openwashdata received the National Prize for Open Research Data from the Swiss Academies of Arts and Sciences. It came early, when we were still arguing that WASH data was worth publishing at all.
    
  
    The community started around 2021 at Global Health Engineering, ETH Zurich, received its first funding in 2023 and has been publishing data packages, running a free course and training data stewards with partners since. Meet the team.
